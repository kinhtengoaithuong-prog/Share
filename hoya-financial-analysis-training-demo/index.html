Chapter 1 · The concept

What you just used

Fixed cost and variable cost

Fixed cost stays the same within the relevant range: y = a.

Variable cost moves in direct proportion to activity: y = bx.

The flexed budget only works once you know which is which.

Fixed budget and flexed budget

The fixed budget is the original plan at planned output. The flexed budget is that plan revised to actual output.

Actual vs flexed: how well we ran the business. Flexed vs fixed: what volume alone was worth.

Volume should have added +6,725. Prices gave back 25,687. Production clawed back 18,388. The price story goes to Sales. The cost story is the next three chapters.

Cost driver and cost rate

A cost driver determines the size of an activity's cost. The cost rate is the price of one unit of that driver.

For electricity: kWh on the bill is the driver, price per kWh is the rate. Usage variance lives in the driver; price variance lives in the rate.

Chapter 4 · The concept

Two lenses before you blame anyone

Controllable and uncontrollable cost

Controllable cost can be influenced by management's decisions. Uncontrollable cost cannot, at least within the period; depreciation is the classic example. Hold managers to account for the first group.

Incurred cost and allocated cost

Incurred: arises this period, like material consumed this month. Allocated: spread from an amount spent earlier, like prepaid rent released month by month. A variance in an allocated line often traces back to the original payment.

Nối lại với bảng overhead: rental và depreciation là allocated; staff, electricity là incurred.

Chapter 4 · The concept

Four reasons a cost can vary

Performance: people did better or worse.

Out-of-date standard: the benchmark is wrong, not the operation.

Random variation: noise that evens out.

Measurement error: the number itself is wrong.

Rule out the last three before blaming the first.

Techniques that work on any expense

Break it into smaller parts. Compare over time. Use ratios. Reconcile back to the assumption behind it: usage × rate, headcount × salary, area × rent.

Two invoice periods start in 2013 and end in 2014. Data quality is part of the analysis.

Chapter 5

Profit is on the P&L. Where is the cash sitting?

Stock on the shelf and money owed by customers are profit you cannot spend yet.

Working capital

The net current assets available for day-to-day operations.

Working capital = current assets − current liabilities

Three components do most of the work: inventory, receivables (AR) and payables (AP). The goal is balance: enough to run smoothly, not so much that cash is locked up.

Chuyển từ P&L sang tiền (T3). Hỏi: "Tháng này X Company lãi 44,786. Tiền mặt có tăng 44,786 không?"

Chapter 5 · The concept

About five months of cover, delivered every two

How to analyse inventory

Break it into categories: material, tools and supplies, semi-finished, finished goods. Age each item: today − receipt date. Calculate cover per item or group on 3, 6 and 12 months of consumption.

What holding stock costs

Holding: capital, warehousing, deterioration, obsolescence, insurance, pilferage. Procuring: ordering, delivery. Shortage: lost contribution, emergency buying, lost production. Too little stock is not free either.

Câu hỏi cho HOYA: "Vật tư nào của mình đang có cover > 3 tháng? Ai quyết định mức đặt hàng?"

Chapter 5 · Reference

When demand and lead time are uncertain

Re-order level = max usage × max lead time Maximum inventory = re-order level + re-order qty − (min usage × min lead time) Buffer safety stock = re-order level − (average usage × average lead time) Average inventory = buffer safety stock + re-order qty ÷ 2

EOQ answers "how much". The re-order level answers "when". The buffer is what uncertainty costs you, paid in stock.

Chapter 5 · The concept

Receivables and payables

AR management

Assess before granting credit: customer history, a credit rating system. Monitor after: the aged receivables listing, bad-debt reports and the level of bad debt.

AP management

Get satisfactory credit terms, stretch them when cash is short, keep good relations with regular and important suppliers. Stretching damages the last one: a trade-off, not free cash.

Nếu còn thời gian: hỏi HOYA đang dùng báo cáo aging nào, ai đọc, bao lâu một lần.

Chapter 6 · The concept

72 + 90 − 60 = 102 days

This company collects cash in 72 days, holds stock for 90, and pays suppliers in 60. 102 days of operations are financed from its own pocket.

That is Chapter 5 in one number.

How to read any ratio

Trend: the same ratio over time. Benchmark: against the industry norm. For working capital, add sales ÷ average working capital.

Nối vòng: CCC là con số tóm tắt của chương 5, margin là tóm tắt chương 1–4.
